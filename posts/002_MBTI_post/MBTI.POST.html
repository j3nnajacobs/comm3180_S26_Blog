Introduction:

Everyone has that extroverted friend who is always smiling and saying hi to everyone. Their energy literally pulls people to them because they are so postive. It makes me think about if extroverts are really that naturally positive.

Alternatively, introverts tend to be more reserved and quiet. Don’t get me wrong, we love out introverted friends, but they can leave you questioning what is really going on in their head.

Then you throw social media in there and the whole thing gets even more complicated. Anyone can post online behind a screen. The social dynamic is gone. It no longer leaves as much effect as a a physical social interaction, for extroverts and introverts.

Investigation:

To see if there is any sort of significance to this phenomenon online, I looked at tweets posted by MBTI identified individuals online.

I used Jupyter Python programming to run a sentiment analysis on all the tweets in this data set extroverts and introverts had posted online.

Extroverts:

After running a sentiment anlysis, I took the compound total of each tweet and averaged all the compounds together. I also calculated the highest and lowest scoring compound tweet that was present for extroverts.

Comparison between Extroverts and Introverts Positivity Online

This graph shows the comparison of the average compound of Extrovert vs. Introverts. There doesn’t seem to be a stark difference of compound value. Although, despite acknowledging that, it’s interesting introverts have a slightly more negative compound.

Conclusion:

After comparing the mean compound of each MBTI group, it appears that there is not strong difference in positivity online. This suggests that, regardless of social orientation, individuals tend to avoid posting content that attracts negative attention. This can be especially true given the fact that anything you post online can trule never go away.

This finding also makes sense when you correlate the minimum and maximum compounds of introverts and extroverts. They came out the same, leading me to believe even in extreme cases, there are similarities of the MBTI types.
